# Disclosure-attack tests

These tests attack the privacy filter of shareable mode ([privacy.md](../../docs/spec/privacy.md)) as an adversary would, on documents that the R package writes, and fail when a document gives back a value that privacy.md undertakes to hide. They run in CI on every pull request that touches the R package, which holds the filter, the writer of findings, the IR hash and the validator, the specification, the privacy cases of the audit corpus that the regression attacks read, the pathology zoo, whose stored instances and generators the tests read, or these tests (`.github/workflows/disclosure.yaml`), and never inside `R CMD check`. Nothing here ships with either package. The history of the tests, the channels they found and the rules that closed them, is in [docs/provenance.md](../../docs/provenance.md).

## Threat model

The adversary of privacy.md holds one shareable results document. They know the package, the registry and the specifications, so they know every definition, rule and detail of suppression, and they can test candidate values against an unkeyed hash, such as the IR hash. They know the values that a predictor can take, its recording grid or the set of its values, but not which rows hold them. They cannot invert a keyed commitment. They do not hold the data or a second document of the same data, against which privacy.md (Guarantees) makes no promise. They know the declaration: the document names the disclosure unit in `/privacy/disclosure_unit` and writes the hash of the declaration unkeyed in `/inputs/declaration`, against which a guessed declaration can be tested, and privacy.md (Guarantees) takes the possible values of a hidden value over the tables and declarations whose documents read alike, so that in effect the declaration is fixed. The guarantee does not extend to a reader who knows a hidden design-structure value from another source, such as the pupils of each school from a published protocol (privacy.md, Guarantees), and the tests model no such reader.

privacy.md protects two tiers of values (Tiers), as the maintainer decided on 11 November 2026 ([02-decisions.md](../../docs/design/02-decisions.md), Q5). A value is outcome-derived, tier A, when it would change if only the outcome column or its trials column changed, their values or which rows lack them: every statistic, support count, status and message of a finding of a T1 or T2 check, the quantities of the analysis sample and the difference between a quantity of the analysis sample and the same quantity of another sample. A value is a design-structure value, tier B, when it depends only on the predictors, keys, labels, grouping columns, assignments and the declaration: every value of a finding of a T0 check and the quantities of the table, the design sample and the rows of assignments and pairs.

The guarantees read a document through its strict reduction (`dh_reduce(strict = TRUE)`), without the time of creation, the digests of keyed commitments and the values that privacy.md accepts to show: the statuses, messages, categories, booleans and names of the T0 checks, the verdict of outcome.separation with its status, message and both_classes, which the verdict and the counts that its finding writes give, the availability of a finding that is not available, the suppression as a whole of outcome.separation, the class of a by_unit count (`dh_strict_finding()` writes a non-person by_unit count below k as if suppressed below k, which would otherwise show the class), the boolean `dominated` and a quantile implied by a written count or shared by k or more of its values. The message of a T0 finding or of outcome.separation renders statistics that the finding writes beside it, and names, so the strict reduction leaves it out whole. A hidden value is one that the document does not write and that no written count measures, and the tables whose documents have the same strict reduction leave it a set of possible values. In these terms:

- A hidden outcome-derived value has two possible values or more, unless every table of the design structure gives it one, as a difference of 0 does when the larger sample's quantity is 0, or the value of a count that the document writes, as the analysed levels of a disclosure unit whose members each have one row take the value of the analysed rows, or it is of the class kept-levels (The attacks, (a)). A hidden outcome-derived count whose detail is not 'min_count', 'min_count_rule' or 'dominance' has a possible value of k or more, unless the bounds that details state keep it below k or rule 1 of Across findings hides it as a difference below k, which its firing displays as 'min_count' would. The adversary may use any inference, through design-structure values too.
- A hidden design-structure count keeps two possible values or more under the linear relations of the document: sums, differences and parts of totals that definitions state, the quantities that several counts measure, with the offsets by which the counts of columns measure levels where the formula fixes them, the same quantity in nested samples, the numerator and denominator of a ratio, the bounds that details state and every count at least 0. Every other inference about design-structure values is residual risk (privacy.md, Guarantees, Residual risk), which these tests report without failing.

The harness classifies every target by this definition in one function, `dh_tier()` (`R/harness.R`): a statistic, support count or status by the tier of its check, T1 and T2 tier A and T0 tier B, a quantity of the analysis sample and a difference from it tier A, a quantity of another sample tier B, and a parameter of a family by the tier that the family states. A hidden value without a tier is an error of the harness, and so is a parameter whose stated tier the tables of its family contradict: one of tier B that differs between two tables that differ only in the outcome column and its trials column, or one of tier A that never does although it takes two values (`dh_parameter_tier_issues()`). Every run of attacks (a) and (b) applies both tests to every table of every family.

## The k of the runs

privacy.md (Parameters) claims its guarantees for every k of 3 or more, and `spec/tools/compile_registry.py` refuses a registry whose min_count is below 3 (E1409). The rules of privacy.md take k as a parameter, so the harness sets min_count in the cached registry, which the filter, the document's `/privacy` and the validator all read, and the package needs no argument for it (`dh_set_k()`). The families run at k = 3, the smallest k for which the guarantees are claimed, so that every determination they report is one that the specification must answer, and so that tables of a few dozen rows reach the rules beside a shown analysis sample, rules 2 and 3 and the quantile rule, which k = 10 reaches only with hundreds of rows. four-sizes runs at k = 4, since written singletons fewer than the position of a quantile bound it from below by 2, which the bound k − 1 of the detail 'min_count' meets at k = 3 only. At k = 3, Decisions suppresses as a whole every finding of a T1 or T2 check below the minimum count when a finding on the analysis sample writes a by_unit count of class non_person, which shows two contributors or more, so the families never see such a finding written beside one, while at k = 10 the written values never bound the contributors by k − 1. The stored instances of the zoo and attacks (c) and (v) run at the registry's k of 10, apart from the regression attacks that set their own: R8-non-person-whole and R12-class-contributors at k = 3, R12-reduced-level at k = 3 and k = 10, R12-ratio-rows at k = 9, since the bound that constant_share places on the analysed rows meets k − 1 only at an odd k, and R13-class-zeros, R13-dominated-contributors and R13-levels-contributors at k = 3, 4 and 5, the k at which each bound on the contributors meets k − 1.

## The attacks

(a) Inference by consistency search (`R/consistency.R`, `R/families.R`, `R/zoo.R`). A family is a template of small tables whose free parameters are counts: cell sizes, events and missing values. The search audits every table of the family in shareable mode, reduces each document to what the adversary can use (`dh_reduce()`: the time of creation and the digests of keyed commitments are dropped, unkeyed hashes kept) and groups the tables whose reduced documents are identical. For each value that the internal findings hold and the shareable document hides, and for each parameter that counts persons and is below k, it reports a determination when the value is the same across a group while the family lets it vary among the tables that hide it with the same detail. Beside the hidden values of the findings, its targets of tier A include the differences between the rows, the contributors and, for each grouping factor, the levels and the levels with two rows or more of the table or the design sample and those of the analysis sample, hidden unless counts write both quantities (`dh_difference_targets()`), since a design-structure count determined beside a written count of the analysis sample gives the difference back.

A determination that only the full reduction makes is accepted, unless it fixes, outside the class kept-levels, a value that rule 1 or 3 of Across findings exists to hide (`dh_rule_hides()`): the rows, the contributors and the levels or levels with two rows of a grouping factor in the analysis sample, their differences from the table or the design sample and the parameters of tier A. privacy.md (Guarantees) asks the filter to close such an inference with a narrow rule where one exists, so the search gives it to the alternatives of the family, which compare full reductions for it, and reports it as a candidate in every family, gated or not, when none traces it. Such a candidate never fails the run, since privacy.md states the guarantee in the strict reduction. The search also accepts a quantile shared by k or more of its values, a value that the design forces in every table of its group, such as the statistics of outcome.constant_clusters for a factor whose every level holds one row (`dh_forced_targets()`), and a count that written counts give through a relation of their definitions: suppress_with, a count of a quantity that another count writes, events and non_events of a finding suppressed as a whole that another finding writes on the same rows, both_classes of outcome.separation beside them, the analysed rows of a bernoulli outcome as events plus non_events (`dh_rows_from_events()`) and the rows and contributors of a sample whose contributors have the basis rows. A determination of the strict reduction is a leak when its target is of tier A and residual risk when it is of tier B, since the search cannot tell a linear determination from another.

A family may give the search its alternatives (`dh_family(alternatives =)`): a function that builds, from the parameters and the case of a table, tables outside the family, each with the values of the family's parameters of tier A that describe it. `dh_alternatives()` audits the alternatives of every table at which the strict reduction determines a value, or the full reduction alone a value of rule 1 or 3 outside the class, and a determination whose target takes another value in an alternative with the same reduction of its own mode is traced: the report counts it by family and alternative, and it does not fail the run. The alternatives are never reported themselves, as the widened tables are not.

A determination of tier A that no alternative traces is of the class kept-levels, which privacy.md (Guarantees) accepts, when the predicate `dh_kept_levels()` (`R/kept.R`) holds in every table of its group, and the report counts it apart. The predicate reads the run, never the family's name, and checks three conditions. First, the value shows that none is lost: a difference of 0 between the design sample and the analysis sample, or a count of the analysis sample equal to the same count of the design sample. Second, the target measures the levels of a grouping factor, its levels with two rows or more or the contributors, so that the number lost is the number of levels or members without any outcome or with fewer than two analysed rows: it is a difference of the design sample from the analysis sample (`dh_difference_targets()`), or a count of a finding on the analysis sample that measures one of these quantities, support.distinct among them. Third, those levels or members are not the rows themselves: the factor has a level of two rows or more in the design sample, and the contributors have neither the basis rows nor a disclosure unit each of whose members holds one row nor, with the basis grouping_factors, a factor of the fewest levels whose levels hold one row each. The rows, the difference of the table from the analysis sample, a status and every count of another quantity lie outside the class whatever their value, and so does a count of the class whose value shows that something was lost. How the document shows the value does not matter, so the predicate needs no bound on the rows without an outcome or on the rows of a level.

A determination within a family is meaningful only when the family lets the value vary for reasons that the document does not show. Four things ensure this:

- Each family varies independently the counts on which the values that it probes depend (`probe`), including levels of one, two and three rows, rows outside the design sample and outcomes missing in part of a level and in whole levels, since a family in which every level has two rows tells the adversary what no document shows. A family probes only the checks whose counts it varies, and places its zeros, events and missing outcomes so that the written T1 statistics do not follow the parameters that it gates. Reals that depend on predictor values are masked unless every predictor of the family takes two values whose counts are parameters, since the adversary does not know which rows hold the values of a covariate.
- Only tables strictly inside every range that is a limit of the family, rather than a natural bound such as 0, are reported.
- A family whose determinations of outcome-derived values in the strict reduction are not all traced, accepted or closed has gate FALSE: they are reported as candidates and do not fail the run. The determinations of the full reduction that no alternative traces are candidates in every family (The attacks, (a)). The gate applies to attack (a) only, and a leak of attack (b), which reads one document and no family, fails the run in every family.
- When the search finds a leak, it runs the family again widened by one value beyond every such limit (`dh_search()`, `--widen`). The widened tables can join a group and remove a determination that only the limits of the family made. A determination that survives is reported with its reproducer.

The search cannot find a leak in a structure that no family holds, and a narrow family can still report a determination that a richer one would remove: each reported leak should be confirmed by the interval audit or by hand.

(b) Interval audit (`R/intervals.R`). From one shareable document alone, the adversary collects the relations that the specification fixes between its counts: parts of a total, sums and differences that definitions state, the quantities that several counts measure in one sample, the nesting of the table, the design sample, the analysis sample and the rows of assignments and pairs, the levels with one and with two or more rows, the contributors of the basis grouping_factors as the fewest levels, the contributors of the basis rows as the rows, the numerators and denominators of written ratios and the bounds that a detail implies: 'min_count' a value below k, and 'secondary' on `support.contributors.distinct` a value of k or more, as validation reads it. 'secondary' on any other count shows nothing about the side of k, since the rules after step 3 give it in place of 'min_count'. Relations that are not linear are marked `structural` (`iv_add()`): rows against levels and levels with two rows, the positions and order of quantiles, a quantile of 1 against the singletons, combinations against their parts, duplicate rows against duplicated keys, informative rows against informative levels, the finer factor's levels against the coarser and informative levels, spellings against labels, zeros against the levels of zeros and rows against contributors. In the full model it adds the conditions of the decision rule that a status shows, for the conditions of the form 'X is above 0.' and 'X is 0.', and the labels in several combinations, which bound a part to 0 only where the relation shows nesting. For the table and the design sample, it adds the difference of each quantity from the analysis sample's as a target of tier A, apart from the levels with one row, which rule 1 does not compare (`iv_difference_targets()`). A difference of the levels of an item whose counts resolve as non-person, which counts levels of two members or more, is accepted when a written count measures the levels of the larger sample, since privacy.md then writes it as other non-person counts, and is a determination otherwise. The rows, contributors and, for each item, levels, levels with two rows or more and levels with one row of the analysis sample that no written count measures are targets of tier A too (`iv_quantity_targets()`). It adds the bounds that a rule implies when it does not fire, as structural relations: a written part of a hidden person total shows that the total exceeds it, and the sum of the written parts, by k or more (`iv_part_bounds()`), and in `iv_firing_bounds()` a quantity of the table or the design sample that a written count measures beside a shown analysis sample exceeds the analysis sample's by k or more (rule 1), for the rows, the contributors and the levels and levels with two rows of an item whose counts resolve as person counts, written analysed levels of a grouping factor whose column is a categorical variable of a term, or whose columns are the categorical variables of a term, are exceeded by k or more by those of the design sample and the table (the end of rule 1), a written count of the levels with one row of a person item, or of those with two rows, shows the other part to be k or more (rule 2), a written real of a finding on the table or the design sample beside a shown analysis sample shows that its rows, and the levels of a person item that the real reads, exceed the analysis sample's by k or more (rule 5), and a written count of columns over the design sample shows that no person item of its terms whose levels a count measures there has fewer than k (rule 6). Each level of an item whose counts resolve as non-person holds two members or more, which bounds the rows by twice the levels and, with the basis disclosure_unit, the contributors too where no member lies in two levels, as privacy.md (Across findings, rule 1) assumes of every such item. With the basis disclosure_unit, the contributors of a sample are its levels of the disclosure unit that the document names, where a grouping factor has the unit's columns, and when the observation key consists of those columns and keys.duplicates writes the message unique, the rows of every sample equal those levels and contributors, a relation that reads a T0 message, which privacy.md accepts, so that only the full program holds it (`iv_unit_relations()`). The analysis sample counts as shown when a finding of a T1 or T2 check is available, or when none is and a real has the detail 'secondary' while every statistic that its suppress_with names is written, which only rule 5 gives and only beside a shown analysis sample (`iv_shown()`). The analysis sample is then the rows of one of the assignments or pairs on which an available finding lies, whose quantities are its own, through binary variables when there are several (`iv_analysis_rows()`). A document whose T0 findings on rows that are the analysis sample show it, as privacy.md (Across findings) lets them, but in which no real shows rule 5, leaves the adversary unable to tell whether it is shown, and a document that is not shown bounds nothing, so the audit applies no bound of rules 1 and 5 to it, which is all that the document lets the adversary use: in the family assign, 186 of the 546 tables of the widened grid are such documents. The counts of columns enter through the terms of the formula, with the kind and coding of their variables (`iv_shape()`, from the IR, which the formula and the values a predictor can take give the adversary), for the categorical variables that are the columns of a grouping factor (`iv_column_relations()`). df of design.collinearity of a main effect of one categorical variable of L levels in the design sample is L - 1 when no earlier main effect has a kept column, and otherwise lies between L - 1 less the df of the earlier main effects and L - 1. kept of design.rank_deficiency is at least the sum of the df of the main effects, plus 1 when the fixed part spans a column of ones, and equals it when every fixed term is a main effect. columns of design.rank_deficiency, dimension of identifiability.levels_vs_dimension and columns of identifiability.residual_confounding are the columns of their terms in the levels that the IR gives their variables, at least those of the table. budget of identifiability.df_budget and design_df of assignment.design_df lie between their levels or units less kept and less 1. The equalities, whose offset the formula fixes, are linear, and the bounds of an offset that the data set are structural. Linear programs solved with lpSolve, with binary variables where a relation chooses between alternatives, then give the smallest and largest value of every hidden count, rounded inwards to integers, which keeps every integer solution, in two programs: the strict program, without the relations that read accepted values, and the linear program, which also leaves out the structural relations.

- A target of tier A with one value in the strict program is a leak, unless it is a difference of 0 that the design forces because the larger sample's quantity is 0, a difference of non-person levels written beside each other, a quantity of the analysis sample of k or more that the written values alone give or of 0 that the design forces, or an accepted channel explains it. A target of tier A that the strict program shows below k is a leak when the program without the bounds of details still shows it below k (`iv_bound_without_details()`), since a bound that only details give shows no more than the report's '<' k of the counts that carry them.
- A target of tier B is a leak when the linear program gives it one value, and residual risk when only the structural relations do.
- An interval that excludes the internal value is an error of the harness.

A by_unit count written below k with the class non_person enters the strict program as a value below k, as the strict reduction writes it, and only the full program reads its value. The test of whether the written values alone give a target (`iv_written_point()`) keeps with the target the hidden counts that measure its quantity, so that the analysed rows that events and non_events of a bernoulli outcome give count as written beside a hidden support.rows, while their difference from the design sample stays a target.

It runs on every distinct document of the families. It still leaves out reals, products of hidden counts, such as the combinations of crossed columns and empty_cells of design.rank_deficiency, the conditions of decision rules of other forms, the bounds that rules 3 and 4 imply when they do not fire, those that rule 1 implies through the sizes of the levels and the chain of samples that lose a quantity, those of the end of rule 1 for the combinations of crossed columns and the rows of assignments, and those of rule 5 on the levels with two rows, whose comparison depends on a count that may be 0, the upper bounds that the sizes of the levels place on the analysed levels, the counts of columns of a term whose variable is no grouping factor of the formula, such as a declared unit that the formula uses only in a fixed term, whose levels the model ties to no item, the items of the fixed terms in the rows of an assignment, which design_df counts there, the offsets of the counts of columns beyond the bounds above, and the bounds that the classes, written counts of levels and `dominated` of the findings of the T1 and T2 checks below the minimum count place on their rows and contributors. The firing channels are gated by the families chain and whole-lost and by the pair attacks of (c), the channels through the columns of a term beside the analysis sample and the sizes of the levels by the regression attacks of rounds 8 and 10, rule 6 of Across findings, which suppresses the counts of columns beside the levels that the filter hides, by R8-column-levels and the attacks of round 9, and the bounds that the availability, status, constant_share, classes and `dominated` of the findings below the minimum count place on their rows and contributors by the attacks of rounds 12 and 13.

## The report

`run.R` prints one line for each leak with a reproducer, the family and its parameters or the attack, then the errors of the harness, the candidates of the families of gate FALSE and, in every family, those of the full reduction, whose target ends with '(full reduction)', the determinations of the class kept-levels under KEPT-LEVELS, one line for each family and target with the number of tables and a reproducer, and, for each family, the number of determinations that each alternative traces, and exits with status 1 when there is a leak or an error. Its summary line counts the determinations of the class among the accepted ones and on their own. A section headed 'Residual risk' follows, with one line for each attack, place and target that determines a design-structure value by an inference other than the linear relations, the number of tables and a reproducer. These lines never fail the run, and they show what privacy.md (Guarantees, Residual risk) leaves open, such as quantiles pinned by the rows and levels, counts of levels with two rows and their parts that the rows and levels give and what the four regression attacks of tier B recover. `dh_known_channels()` lists the channels of outcome-derived values, or linear relations of design-structure values, that privacy.md records as not closed, and the report lists apart, under KNOWN, their leaks and the candidates that they explain in families of gate FALSE. Each channel lists its places, each target with the table of its family or the regression attack, so that a determination elsewhere, even of a listed target, is reported as a leak or a candidate, and a listed place that no attack finds is an error of the harness when its family or attack runs. The three kinds of determinations that privacy.md named as not yet covered left the list on 14 November 2026, when they joined the class kept-levels. It holds one channel, crossed-product, the inference of R14-crossed-product and R14-crossed-key-product that privacy.md (Guarantees) and docs/design/03-execution-plan.md (M2, Decisions that bind later milestones) accept.

## The Python package

The attacks reach the filter of the Python package by parity rather than by a port of the harness, since attack (a) reads the internal findings through the R namespace to know which values a document hides, and every attack sets k in the cached registry of the R package. A Python document that agrees with the R document gives back no more than the R document, so the determinations that attacks (a), (b) and (c) make on the R documents cover it, and a leak of the Python filter shows as a difference.
