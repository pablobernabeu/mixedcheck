## Claims checked

1. review-catalogue.md (L4 entry on variance-component tests): likelihood ratio statistics for parameters on the boundary have chi-bar-square (mixture) null distributions; with more than one parameter on the boundary, or a nuisance parameter on it, the weights depend on the information matrix and are not 50:50; and Self and Liang give a case that is not a chi-square mixture at all. The verifier could not confirm the last point.
   **Confirmed, the last point included.** The abstract (p. 605) states that the paper derives the asymptotic distributions of maximum likelihood estimators and likelihood ratio statistics when the true value may lie on the boundary, that the problem is asymptotically equivalent to estimating the restricted mean of a multivariate Gaussian from one observation, that several special cases give mixtures of chi-squared distributions, and that one example, with a nuisance parameter on the boundary, has an asymptotic distribution that is not a mixture of chi-squared distributions. The representation (pp. 606–607) projects a Gaussian vector with covariance equal to the inverse information onto the cone of the parameter space, which is why the mixing weights depend on the information matrix except in the simplest cases.
2. open_items_local_session.csv: 'Asymptotics of boundary LRTs (V4 comparator rationale)'. **Supported.** The paper's results hold under conditions like Cramér's for independent, identically distributed observations (Introduction, p. 605); Crainiceanu and Ruppert (2004) explain that this restricts them to mixed models whose data split into many independent, identically distributed subvectors, which is the reason V4 compares the mixture with exact and bootstrap tests.
