## Each shareable rule of Q5 (02-decisions.md) against the guidance

1. **k = 10 members per displayed cell, bin or band.** In line. The SRS's threshold is 10 data subjects; counts below 10, and statistics from groups of fewer than 10, are suppressed, and zeros count as below the threshold (ONS SRS p. 7). ONS–DfE keeps the default of 10 for counts of pupils, learners and teachers, which data owners may raise or lower (pp. 4–5). The Handbook uses 10 throughout (p. 98) and applies it to frequencies and to the mean, median, mode, standard deviation and variance (p. 32). SACRO notes that 3 is the only value with a clear statistical justification and that most organisations use 5 or 10, leaving the value to the data owner (p. 7). All four describe principles-based checking, in which the rules are rules of thumb and a dataset's own rules or an agreed exemption take precedence (ONS SRS p. 7; ONS–DfE p. 5; Handbook p. 24); the suite's filter applies its rules without exception and so is the stricter reading.
2. **Dominance: the two largest contributors supply less than 90% of a bin's rows.** The parameters match SACRO's worked example of the N–K rule (top T = 2, k% = 90) beside a p-ratio rule with p% = 10, both to be set by the data owner (p. 48). The SRS defines dominance differently: the largest entity above 43.75% of the total, or all entities but the two largest together below 12.5% of the largest (ONS SRS p. 18). The SRS's first condition can fail where Q5's rule passes: a bin in which the largest contributor holds 50% of the rows and the second 10% passes Q5 (60% < 90%) and fails the SRS (50% > 43.75%). SACRO treats dominance as a check for magnitudes, not frequencies (p. 7), so applying it to the rows of a bin is, as the record says, this project's adaptation. ONS–DfE states no dominance rule.
3. **A quantile p is shown only if n·min(p, 1 − p) ≥ k.** In line for the usual sets of quantiles. The SRS suppresses a median below 2k observations, quartiles below 4k, deciles below 10k and the 1st and 99th percentiles below 100k, reasoning from the share of the group that each quantile corresponds to (ONS SRS pp. 24–25); these are the bounds that Q5's rule gives for those quantiles. SACRO asks for at least the threshold on either side of a percentile (p. 84) and the Handbook for at least the threshold behind each reported percentile value (p. 35). ONS–DfE is stricter for irregular sets: each reported percentile must rest on at least 10 observations between it and the next (p. 12), and the SRS's reasoning implies the same, so the 0.50 and 0.55 quantiles of 120 rows pass Q5's rule and fail both. ONS–DfE also treats minima and maxima as disclosive unless at least 10 individuals share the value (p. 12); Q5 shows no minima or maxima.
4. **Exact counts of non-person units, parameters and df.** For models, all four sources ask for enough residual degrees of freedom: stated and at least the threshold in the SRS (ONS SRS p. 39), more than 10 in ONS–DfE (p. 11), at least the threshold in the Handbook (p. 68) and a minimum of 10 since Brandt et al. (2010) in SACRO (p. 7). The SRS adds that a model must not rest on one unit or be saturated (p. 39). Sequential regressions must not differ by fewer than the threshold in observations (ONS–DfE p. 11; Handbook p. 68), and no output may allow differencing (ONS SRS p. 8), which Q5's comparison of samples (privacy.md, Across findings, rule 1) covers. Showing df exactly is in line. Counts of non-person units are another matter. The SRS's general rules count organisations, such as schools and firms, as data subjects, so that a count of fewer than 10 schools is suppressed (p. 7), although its section on frequency tables gives a threshold of 3 for properties and companies (p. 10). ONS–DfE accepts counts of a type of school at national, regional or local-authority level only when they are zero or at least 3, does not allow institutions to be identified under the Digital Economy Act, and allows an identified institution's count of individuals only at 10 or more (pp. 5–6). Exact counts of schools or classes in a shareable report can therefore fail the SRS's default rules, which the record leaves to the providers' rules applying on top of the defaults.
5. **Suppressed tables rounded to base 5 with margins recomputed, or secondary suppression.** In line. The SRS requires secondary disclosure control wherever a suppressed value could be recovered by differencing (pp. 8 and 10). ONS–DfE requires totals to be recomputed as the sum of the components after suppression (p. 5) and, for data linked to higher-education records, all counts of people rounded to the nearest 5, with counts below 2.5 shown as 0 (p. 11). SACRO requires totals to be adjusted after suppression, noise or rounding, or controlled rounding or secondary suppression (p. 35).
6. **No row-level values, minima, maxima, outliers or point plots.** In line. The SRS never permits record-level data (p. 7) and classes estimation residuals and graphs of actual data as unsafe, while regression coefficients and test statistics are safe (p. 7). ONS–DfE discourages residual plots and prefers a written description or a test (pp. 11–12).
7. **Zeros in cross-tabulations of person attributes are disclosive.** In line with the SRS, which treats zeros as a disclosure risk unless they are structural and evident as such from the request, and treats full cells (100% of a group) as a class-disclosure risk to be judged case by case (pp. 10 and 13). Stricter than ONS–DfE's general rule, which permits counts of zero unless a project's data schedule says otherwise (p. 5), although the class-disclosure rule of the same policy suppresses empty and full cells unless both checkers judge them structural (p. 13). SACRO distinguishes structural zeros, which are safe, from informative ones and adds evidential zeros, cells that could hold a value but would be expected to be empty (Appendix 3, p. 90).
8. **Combining sources and dataset rules.** When an output draws on several sources, the strictest rules of those in scope apply (ONS–DfE p. 5), and dataset-specific rules set by a data owner replace or add to the SRS's defaults (ONS SRS p. 7).

## Bearing on Q5 and the Open checks

The defaults agree with the primary UK guidance on the threshold, the usual quantiles, residual df, differencing, rounding and point-level detail, and with the SRS on zeros. Three points differ, each where the guidance is stricter, and are for the maintainer:

- **Dominance.** The SRS's rule (largest above 43.75%, or the rest below 12.5% of the largest) differs from Q5's (2, 0.90) and catches a single large contributor that Q5's rule passes.
- **Quantiles.** For irregular sets of quantiles, ONS–DfE and the SRS's reasoning require the threshold between adjacent reported quantiles, which n·min(p, 1 − p) ≥ k does not.
- **Counts of non-person units.** The SRS's default rules treat schools and firms as data subjects with a threshold of 10, and ONS–DfE sets 3 for counts of a type of school; Q5 shows such counts exactly.

All three arise only where a provider's rules apply, which Q5 already makes binding. Registry options that switch to the SRS's dominance rule, the spacing rule for quantiles and a threshold for organisational units would let a user of SRS data meet them without editing the filter.
