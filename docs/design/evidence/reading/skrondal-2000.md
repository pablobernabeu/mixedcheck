## Claims checked

1. open_items_local_session.csv: 'Metamodel analysis of fractional or Latin-hypercube grids (V1, V5)'.
   **Partly supported.** The article argues against the conventional design of Monte Carlo experiments (no explicit metamodel, case studies or full factorial designs, no variance reduction, many replications, results read by eye). In its place it recommends explicit metamodels relating the performance statistics to the experimental conditions, incomplete designs, chiefly fractional factorials, common random numbers to sharpen the metamodel estimates, and fewer replications per condition in exchange for more conditions and so better external validity (abstract, p. 137; the fractional-factorial section; Conclusion). It does not discuss Latin-hypercube designs: neither 'Latin' nor 'hypercube' occurs in the text. The citation supports metamodels for fractional factorial grids only, and a Latin-hypercube grid for V1 or V5 needs another source.
2. Relevant to 02-decisions.md (Q9), which predicts false-flag rates with a metamodel and sets replicate counts from Monte Carlo SE targets (2,000 replicates for rates near .05): the article supports the metamodel, but it argues for fewer replications per condition, spread over more conditions, rather than many replications per cell. The two can be reconciled through the metamodel's precision over the design region, which is where 02 places its simultaneous bands. The article's examples come from structural equation modelling.
