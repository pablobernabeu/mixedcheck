# Barr (2013)

**Reference as checked.** Barr, D. J. (2013). Random effects structure for testing interactions in linear mixed-effects models. *Frontiers in Psychology, 4*, Article 328. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00328

**Match.** Crossref (queried 21 November 2026) and the publisher's page agree on author, title, journal, volume 4, article number 328 and DOI. Published 5 June 2013 as an opinion article. Not retracted or corrected. Crossref lists no licence for it.

**Platform and pages read.** Frontiers, HTML full text (free to read), read 21 November 2026, complete article including Tables 1 to 4. The article is short and unsectioned, so places are given by paragraph and table.

## Claims checked

1. 02-decisions.md, Q3 (protected set): 'for a focal interaction, the slope of the highest-order combination of within-unit factors it contains (Barr, 2013)'.
   **Supported, with one condition left out.** The article's proposed guideline (first paragraph) is that models testing interactions should include random slopes for the highest-order combination of within-unit factors subsumed by each interaction, and it states this for designs with replications, that is, several observations per sampling unit per cell. Without replication, slope variance cannot be told apart from error variance and the slope need not be included. The closing paragraph adds that including only the critical slope was enough for acceptable Type I error (the 'BC' model in the 2 × 2 × 2 design). 02 states the rule without the condition on replication; the condition matters for designs with one observation per unit and cell.

2. 03-execution-plan.md, Open checks: 'the Type I error figures for interactions'; reports.json (research:re-structure, recommendation 2): 'intercepts-only gave .170-.267 for interactions against .063-.064 for the maximal model'.
   **Figures correct but the range is incomplete.** Table 1 (AB interaction, 2 × 2 design, A between, B within) gives Type I error rates for the random-intercept model of .170, .267 and .395 with 4, 8 and 16 replications per cell, against .063, .064 and .063 for the maximal model and .050, .052 and .049 for ANOVA. The quoted range stops at 8 replications and leaves out .395. Table 3 (ABC interaction, 2 × 2 × 2) gives .069 to .197 for random intercepts only, .102 to .241 for the model with slopes for B and C but not BC, .050 to .063 for the BC slope alone and .046 to .062 for the maximal model.

Simulation conditions, for any later use: 10,000 datasets per set and replication level, 24 sampled units, a continuous normal outcome, a single source of random-effect variance (by-unit, not by-item), deviation coding (treatment coding gave identical results), and non-converging fits (about 2%) removed from the analysis.
