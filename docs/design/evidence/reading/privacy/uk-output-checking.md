## Each shareable rule of Q5 (02-decisions.md) against the guidance

1. **k = 10 members per displayed cell, bin or band.** In line. The SRS checks every output against a low-count threshold of 10 by default, data owners may set it higher or lower, and DfE keeps 10 for counts of pupils, learners and teachers (ONS–DfE pp. 4–5). The Handbook uses 10 throughout as the commonly used threshold (p. 89). SACRO notes that 3 is the only value with a clear statistical justification and that most organisations use 5 or 10, as a margin and against differencing, leaving the value to the data owner (p. 7). All three describe a principles-based approach, in which a result below the threshold may still be released when the checker judges its risk negligible (ONS–DfE p. 5; Handbook p. 24); the suite's rules-based filter is the stricter reading.
2. **Dominance: the two largest contributors supply less than 90% of a bin's rows.** The parameters match SACRO's worked example of the N–K rule (top T = 2 observations, k% = 90) beside a p-ratio rule with p% = 10, both parameters to be set by the data owner (p. 48). SACRO treats dominance as a check for magnitudes, not frequencies (p. 7), and does not apply it formally to tests, regressions or shape statistics (p. 89). Applying it to the rows of a bin is therefore, as the record says, this project's adaptation, and goes beyond what the guidance asks. The ONS–DfE policy states no dominance rule.
3. **A quantile p is shown only if n·min(p, 1 − p) ≥ k.** In line with SACRO, which asks for at least the threshold on either side of a percentile (p. 84) and checks percentiles with their complements (p. 42). The ONS–DfE policy is stricter when several quantiles are shown: each percentile must rest on at least 10 observations between it and the next reported value (p. 12). A report that shows two close quantiles, such as the 0.50 and 0.55 quantiles of 120 rows, passes the Q5 rule and fails the SRS rule. The SRS also asks that a mean, median, mode, standard deviation or variance rest on at least 10 observations (7 for linked higher-education data), and treats minima and maxima as disclosive unless at least 10 individuals share the value (p. 12); Q5 shows no minima or maxima.
4. **Exact counts of non-person units, parameters and df.** For models, all three sources ask for enough residual degrees of freedom: more than 10 in the SRS (p. 11), at least the threshold in the Handbook (pp. 63, 66) and a minimum of 10 since Brandt et al. (2010) in SACRO (p. 7). They also ask that sequential regressions not differ by fewer than 10 observations (ONS–DfE p. 11; Handbook p. 63), which Q5's comparison of samples (privacy.md, Across findings, rule 1) covers. Showing df exactly is in line, and the SRS releases standard errors, t statistics and p values once those conditions hold (p. 11). For institutions, DfE accepts counts of a type of school at national, regional or local-authority level only when they are zero or at least 3, does not allow institutions to be identified under the Digital Economy Act, and allows an identified institution's count of individuals only at 10 or more (pp. 5–6). Exact counts of non-person units can therefore conflict with a provider's rule; the record already says that providers' rules apply on top of the defaults.
5. **Suppressed tables rounded to base 5 with margins recomputed, or secondary suppression.** In line. DfE requires totals to be recomputed as the sum of the components after suppression (p. 5) and, for data linked to higher-education records, rounding of all counts of people to the nearest 5, with counts below 2.5 shown as 0 (p. 11). SACRO requires totals to be adjusted after suppression, noise or rounding, or controlled rounding or secondary suppression (p. 35).
6. **No row-level values, minima, maxima, outliers or point plots.** In line. The SRS discourages residual plots, since each residual is usually one observation, and prefers a written description or a test (pp. 11–12).
7. **Zeros in cross-tabulations of person attributes are disclosive.** Stricter than DfE's general rule, which permits counts of zero, structural or not, unless a project's data schedule says otherwise (p. 5), and in line with the class-disclosure rule of the same policy, under which full and empty cells are suppressed unless both checkers judge them structural (p. 13). SACRO distinguishes structural zeros, which are safe, from informative ones and adds a third kind, evidential zeros, cells that could hold a value but would be expected to be empty (Appendix 3, p. 90).
8. **Combining sources.** When an output draws on several sources, the SRS applies the strictest rules of those in scope (p. 5).

## Bearing on Q5 and the Open checks

The defaults agree with the primary UK guidance on the threshold, rounding, residual df, differencing and point-level detail, and are stricter on zeros and on dominance. Two points differ in the other direction and are for the maintainer: the quantile rule, which the SRS sets between adjacent reported percentiles, and exact counts of institutions, which DfE bounds below at 3. Both arise only when the data provider's rules apply, which Q5 already makes binding; a registry option for the SRS percentile rule would close the first for users of DfE data.
