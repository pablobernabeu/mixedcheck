## Claims checked

1. 02-decisions.md, Q3: for nested designs in R, cluster-robust CR2 inference through clubSandwich is available as a terminal alternative (Pustejovsky & Tipton, 2018); registry assignment.unit_in_random_part, remedy cluster_robust: CR2 small-sample correction clustered on the randomisation unit, through clubSandwich; review-catalogue.md, L7-03: CR2 cluster-robust SEs with small-sample degrees of freedom.
   **Supported.** The paper generalises bias-reduced linearisation (CR2) to models with arbitrary sets of fixed effects, pairs it with a Satterthwaite approximation for t-tests and proposes an approximate Hotelling's T² test for multiple-parameter hypotheses; in its simulations the conventional cluster-robust Wald test could over-reject severely while the proposed tests kept Type I error near nominal; the methods are implemented in clubSandwich (Abstract). The Conclusion recommends CR2 with estimated degrees of freedom whenever cluster-robust variance estimation is used. The paper's framework is linear regression with fixed effects estimated by OLS or WLS; it also applies the tests to random-effects (feasible GLS) estimates in the drinking-age example (Section 5.2), which is the closest it comes to the mixed models of the suite.
2. The correction. **Relevant and not yet recorded in the repository.** The corrigendum states that Theorem 2, a computational short cut for CR2 in models with cluster-specific fixed effects, does not hold for models estimated by generalised least squares with inverse working-model weights, and restricts the revised theorem to OLS. The suite applies CR2 to mixed-model (GLS-type) fits through clubSandwich, so the record should cite the correction beside the article and rely on a clubSandwich version released after it; the H0 library has clubSandwich 0.7.0, from the snapshot of 20 November 2026. clubSandwich's source, read on 22 November 2026 in the current CRAN release, 0.7.0 of 4 May 2026 (through CRAN's mirror on GitHub), and in the development version 0.7.0.9000, applies the short cut in `augmented_model_matrix()`, which leaves out the cluster-specific fixed effects that a model absorbs when the weights are inverse-variance and the clustering matches those effects. Only the methods for plm (within models), lm_robust and iv_robust define it. lme and lmerMod fits use the default, which absorbs nothing, so their CR2 adjustment is computed from the full fixed-effects design. The simplified form that `inverse_var` selects is exact for them, since the target is then the inverse of the weights. The corrigendum therefore does not affect the suite's use of CR2.

3. Read in full. The Conclusion names two limits that bear on the terminal state: the tests can under-reject markedly when clusters are very few and the design is unbalanced, especially for hypotheses of several dimensions, and CR2 becomes costly or infeasible when clusters are large, since its adjustment matrices are n_i × n_i (Section 6). The corrigendum's revised theorem holds only for ordinary least squares with a homoscedastic working model (Section 4), which leaves the fits that the suite uses unaffected, as claim 2 records.
