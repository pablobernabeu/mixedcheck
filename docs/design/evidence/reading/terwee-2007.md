## Claims checked

1. spec/registry/checks/outcome.boundary_shares.yaml, thresholds floor and ceiling (value 0.15, descriptive): more than 15% of respondents at the lowest (highest) possible score is a common criterion for a floor (ceiling) effect in the validation of health status questionnaires, where each respondent gives one score; applying it to the rows of an analysis sample is the project's extension. review-catalogue.md (L3 and L7 entries) cites the same criterion.
   **Confirmed.** Section 8 considers floor or ceiling effects present if more than 15% of respondents achieved the lowest or highest possible score. The authors take the criterion from McHorney and Tarlov (1995, Quality of Life Research, 4, 293–307), so Terwee et al. are a secondary source for the 15% figure itself. They give a positive quality rating for the absence of floor and ceiling effects only in a sample of at least 50 patients, a condition the registry entry does not carry. The catalogue's caveat holds: the criterion is a quality rating for health-status questionnaires, not a statistical threshold for choosing a model, and the registry rightly labels its use as descriptive and its application to rows as the project's extension.
