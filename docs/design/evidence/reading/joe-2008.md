## Claims checked

1. review-catalogue.md (L3 and L4 entries on Laplace and quadrature): the asymptotic bias of Laplace estimates is driven mainly by how discrete the response is; it is largest for binary and ordinal outcomes and for counts concentrated near zero, and shrinks as cluster size grows; the Laplace approximation is often adequate even for small clusters, so a flag should stay advisory.
   **Confirmed** (Abstract). The Discussion adds that the Laplace approximation generally does well and better with less discreteness and larger clusters, that its inverse Hessian slightly underestimates the standard errors of dispersion parameters, that the dimension of the random-effect vector seems not to matter apart from near non-identifiability as it grows, and that it is adequate for Poisson models of overdispersed counts but less good for binary and ordinal responses.
2. open_items_local_session.csv: 'Laplace accuracy for discrete mixed models; V9 module G06'. **Supported**, with the author's recommendations for G06 (Section 4): use the Laplace approximation for quick comparisons of competing models; where it drives dependence parameters to the boundary, refit with adaptive Gauss–Hermite quadrature if the number of quadrature points raised to the dimension of the random effects is not too large (the HTML prints the feasible limit as '58', most likely 5⁸ with the superscript lost; the PDF would settle it); and check the best-fitting model's estimates and covariance matrix with adaptive Gauss–Hermite quadrature when that is easy. The results are asymptotic calculations and comparisons on data, not a Monte Carlo study of finite-sample error rates.
