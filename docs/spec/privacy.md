# Privacy filter

The privacy filter turns the findings of a run into the findings of a shareable results document (02-decisions.md, Q5). Every file that leaves the package passes through it unless the caller asks for internal mode explicitly, and a shareable document meets the invariants of [documents.md](documents.md) (Shareable documents, E7020 to E7024). This document specifies the two tiers of values it protects, its input, the order of its steps, how each rule of Q5 applies to a finding, how messages render, how labels become pseudonyms, which channels of disclosure it closes and which inferences it leaves open. It is normative together with documents.md and [registry.md](registry.md) (Statistics and counts, Messages), which also states how by_unit counts resolve. M2 applies it to the findings of the results document. M4 adds figures, their aggregated tables, rounding to `rounding_base` and the secondary suppression of tables, and extends this document.

## Parameters

The filter reads its parameters from the compiled registry (registry.md, The compiled registry) and the results document records them in `/privacy`. Below, k is `min_count`, the dominance rule has `dominance.contributors` contributors and the share m / 10^d written as `dominance.share`, and `zeros_disclosive` true means that a count of 0 is suppressed like any other count below k. The settings of registry 0.1.0 are k = 10, two contributors, the share 0.9 and zeros treated as disclosive, and every rule below that says '0 included' assumes zeros_disclosive true.

k is at least 3, which the registry compiler enforces (registry.md, The compiler). A finding that fails the minimum-count rule keeps the detail 'min_count' on `support.contributors.distinct` (Steps, 3), and a sample with an available finding holds a row, so that it has one contributor or more unless none of its rows names a member of the disclosure unit. At k = 2 that detail would leave its contributors the single value 1, and only the suppression of every such finding as a whole could prevent it. The rules below are stated for every k of 3 or more.

## Tiers

The filter protects two tiers of values (02-decisions.md, Q5, 11 November 2026). A value is outcome-derived when it would change if only the outcome column or its trials column changed, their values or which rows lack them, with every other column, the declaration and the arguments fixed, since a row whose trials are missing or 0 leaves the analysis sample as one without an outcome does (ir.md, Samples): the statistics, supports, statuses, messages and resolved classes of the findings of the T1 and T2 checks, the quantities of the analysis sample (Across findings) and the sizes of its levels, and the difference between a quantity of the analysis sample and the same quantity of another sample. A value is a design-structure value when it depends only on the predictors, keys, labels, grouping columns, assignments and the declaration: the values of the findings of the T0 checks, which read no outcome (registry.md, Tier and inputs), and the quantities of the table, the design sample and the rows of assignments and pairs. A design-structure value that the filter suppresses to protect an outcome-derived value stays a design-structure value.

A hidden outcome-derived value is protected against every inference from the document, through the design-structure values that it writes too. A hidden design-structure value is protected by the minimum-count, dominance, threshold and quantile rules of steps 2 to 5 and against the linear relations of the document, sums, differences and parts of totals, within a finding and across findings and samples. Guarantees states both and lists the inferences about design-structure values that the filter leaves open.

## Input

The filter receives the internal findings of the run in the order of documents.md (Findings), each with:

- the registry entry of its check
- its statistics with their internal values, every by_unit count already resolved to person or non_person (registry.md, Statistics and counts) and every quantile with its p and n
- its support, with the numbers behind the dominance rule: the rows of the sample and the summed rows c of the largest contributors
- the internal message, its identifier and the values of its arguments

and, for the document as a whole, the disclosure unit, the commitment key of the run and the counts of the samples that the rules across findings compare (Across findings). A finding that is not available passes through unchanged, also when the reason rests on a count that the rules across findings suppress, as Decisions accepts. The run resolves every by_unit count before the filter, in internal mode as well, so that every results document records the resolved class.

## Steps

The filter applies these steps in order. A value that a step suppresses becomes null with `availability` not_available, the reason privacy_suppressed and the detail given, and keeps its kind, count class and quantile. A value that an earlier step suppressed keeps its detail, apart from the detail 'min_count', which steps 6 and 7 replace on the counts that their rules suppress (Within a finding and Across findings).

1. Internal statistics. Every statistic that its entry marks internal_only is suppressed, with the detail 'internal_only'.
2. Minimum count and dominance. A finding fails the minimum-count rule when its support has fewer than k distinct contributors, and the dominance rule when c × 10^d ≥ m × rows in integer arithmetic, with c the summed rows of its largest contributors (documents.md, Support). In a finding that fails either, every statistic of kind real, reals or labels and every count and ratio of class person is suppressed, with the detail 'min_count_rule' when the finding fails the minimum-count rule and 'dominance' otherwise (E7021). Counts and ratios of class non_person, booleans, categories and names stay. This step leaves the support to step 3.
3. Person counts below k. Every person count still written that is below k, 0 included, is suppressed with the detail 'min_count' (E7020): statistics of kind count with the class person, `support.rows` and `support.contributors.distinct`. A quantile that step 4 suppresses, every minimum and maximum among them, is left to that step, which suppresses it whatever its value, so that its detail does not show on which side of k it lies. A quartile of at most four values or a median of at most two is the minimum or the maximum, and its detail would otherwise show where that lies. So the detail 'min_count' marks only values below k, and every such value other than a quantile that the quantile rule suppresses and a count that a rule of steps 6 or 7 suppresses too, which takes that rule's detail. A count that a later rule suppresses would otherwise keep 'min_count' when below k, so that the detail 'secondary' would show that it is at least k: beside 10 written levels, multi_row_levels with 'secondary' would then be 10, and the levels with one row 0. support.contributors.distinct keeps 'min_count', which shows that the finding fails the minimum-count rule, as the details of step 2 show too.
4. Quantiles. A statistic with quantile p of n values, n being the internal value of the count that the entry names, is suppressed with the detail 'quantile' unless n × min(m, 10^d − m) ≥ k × 10^d in integer arithmetic, where m / 10^d is p (E7022). A minimum or a maximum, the quantile 0 or 1, is therefore never written.
5. Ratios. A ratio whose numerator or denominator is suppressed, whatever its class, or is a person count below k is suppressed with the detail 'ratio' (E7024). A non-person count that a rule across findings suppresses would otherwise follow from the ratio and the other count: constant_share of outcome.constant_clusters beside its written constant_levels gives back multi_row_levels, the analysed schools of two pupils or more that rule 1 hides as within k of those of the design sample.
6. Relations within a finding. The rules of Within a finding below are applied, and step 5 with them, until no value or detail changes.
7. Across findings. The clauses at the end of rule 1 of Across findings, under which the analysis sample loses a value, and rules 2 and 3 are applied to the analysis sample, and steps 6, 8 and 9 to the findings on it, which decides whether the analysis sample is shown. The rules of Across findings are then applied to the whole document in this order: rule 1 between the analysis sample, when it is shown, and the samples beside it, with the extension of rule 5 to every count of a lost item and the chain clause of rule 1, repeated until nothing changes, then rule 1 between the other samples, rule 2 in every sample other than the analysis sample, and for each finding the counts that measure a hidden quantity, rule 4, rule 5 and rule 6. Step 6 is applied again to every finding that they change.
8. Absent statistics. A statistic that its entry marks internal_only is written as suppressed, with the detail 'internal_only', when its definition leaves it absent, so that its absence shows nothing: bounds and columns_involved of outcome.separation are absent when the programs of bounds, two for each kept column of the analysis sample, number more than max_bound_programs, and their absence would show, beside kept of design.rank_deficiency, that the rows without an outcome removed a kept column. Such a count keeps the class of its entry, and a by_unit one, which resolves over no levels, takes the class person. A ratio that its definition leaves absent is written as suppressed, with the detail 'ratio', when its numerator or denominator is suppressed, and a quantile that its definition leaves absent is written as suppressed, with the detail 'quantile', when the count that gives its n is suppressed. A by_unit statistic written so takes the class to which that suppressed count resolves. Its absence would otherwise show that value, as the absence of constant_share of outcome.constant_clusters shows that multi_row_levels is 0. Entries define their other statistics so that their absence depends on no person count, as effective_size of structure.cluster_sizes, which is 'NaN' for a factor with one level.
9. Decisions. The rules of Decisions below may suppress a finding as a whole.
10. Pseudonyms. Every label still written is replaced by its pseudonym (Pseudonyms).
11. Writing. The quantile of a statistic keeps p and keeps n only when the count that gives n is written in the finding, so that n never shows a suppressed count. Every message is rendered again from the written values (Messages).

The filter never writes a value that the internal document does not hold, and it never changes a status other than availability.

## Within a finding

Three relations that an entry states let a written value give back a suppressed one within the finding (registry.md, Statistics and counts).

- suppress_with. A statistic whose suppress_with names a suppressed statistic is suppressed with the detail 'secondary'. It covers sums and differences, such as rows_kept and rows_dropped of sample.raw_vs_analysis, which add up to the written rows_raw, and a real of a T1 check computed from its counts, such as events_per_column of outcome.minority_events. When a statistic that it names is suppressed with a detail other than 'min_count', or names it in its own suppress_with, the detail 'secondary' also replaces a detail 'min_count' that step 3 gave. A statistic derived from another would otherwise show that it is below k while the detail of the other shows that step 3 found that one at least k: bound of identifiability.levels_vs_dimension, the levels minus 1, with the detail 'min_count' beside levels suppressed with 'secondary' would give the levels as 10. Two statistics that name each other, such as rows_kept and rows_dropped, are parts of a total, and the detail 'min_count' would show which of them lies below k, and when both did, bound each from below by the total minus k − 1: identical_keys and conflicting_keys of keys.duplicates beside 18 duplicated_keys, or events and non_events of outcome.minority_events beside 18 rows, would each be 9. So both parts have the detail 'secondary'.
- part_of. A count whose part_of names a total counts a part of it, and the total minus its parts counts the rest, such as the counts other than 0 beside zeros of outcome.zero_shares. When the total is a person count, every count that names it in part_of is suppressed with the detail 'secondary' if the total minus one of them that steps 1 to 5 left written, or minus the sum of all of them that steps 1 to 5 left written, is below k, 0 included. The rule reads these internal values whether or not a later rule suppresses the total or a part, so that its result does not depend on the order of the rules, as for the nested totals multi_row_levels, varying_levels and informative_levels of structure.within_between. A part below k has the detail 'secondary' too, since the rule gives that detail to parts of k or more, which beside a written total would otherwise bound them. Such a remainder has the class of the total, so a non-person total is shown with its parts exactly.
- Quantiles. A statistic with quantile whose n is given by a suppressed count is suppressed with the detail 'quantile', since the quantile rule cannot then be checked from the document (E7022). The detail 'quantile' also replaces a detail 'min_count' that step 3 gave, since step 3 leaves to step 4 only the quantiles that the quantile rule suppresses, so that 'min_count' would show that the rule held and bound n: a median of structure.cluster_sizes with the detail 'min_count' would show that the factor has at least 20 levels.

## Across findings

A document can give back a suppressed count through values that different findings write, since several checks count the same rows, members or levels in different samples. These rules read the counts of the samples, not the values that other findings write, so that the shareable form of a finding depends only on its own internal values and on the samples.

The samples are the table, the design sample, the analysis sample when the run read the outcome, and the rows of each assignment (registry.md, Terms) and of each pair of columns that structure.label_incidence and structure.id_scope_evidence read, those of the design sample in which both columns are present, on which a check yields an available finding, since no finding writes a quantity of other rows. The rows of an assignment or of a pair lie in the design sample. They are the design sample when they hold the same rows as it, and otherwise the analysis sample when they hold the same rows as that, and the rows of assignments and pairs that hold the same rows as one another are one sample, since a sample with the same rows gives back nothing that it does not. So rule 1 compares two samples with the same rows only among the table, the design sample and the analysis sample. The quantities of a sample are its rows, its contributors as documents.md (Support) counts them and, for each item, the number of its levels, of its levels with at least two rows and of its levels with one row, which rule 1 compares as it states. The items are the grouping factors of the IR, the declared units that do not count as absent and the items by which the by_unit counts of the run's findings resolve (registry.md, Statistics and counts), so that other columns and combinations, such as the observation key and the outcome, are not items. Two items are one item when they divide the rows of the table into the same levels, as the factor school:class, the unit class declared within_parent in school and the pair of the grouping columns school and class do. The findings on the analysis sample are those of the T1 and T2 checks and those of T0 checks whose rows of an assignment or of a pair are the analysis sample. The analysis sample is shown when, after step 7 has applied the clauses at the end of rule 1 and rules 2 and 3 to it and steps 6, 8 and 9 to the findings on it, one of these findings is available and not suppressed as a whole. When it is not, the document writes no value of the analysis sample, so no sample is compared with it, and the design sample writes its rows, levels and reals as a T0 document does: in a gaussian T1 run in which outcome.constant_clusters is suppressed as a whole, every value of the T0 checks that steps 2 to 5 leave written is written.

A count measures a quantity when its entry says so in measures (registry.md, Statistics and counts). `support.rows` measures the rows of the sample that the check's entry names, and `support.contributors.distinct` its contributors and, with the basis grouping_factors, the levels of every grouping factor whose number of levels in that sample equals it, and with the basis disclosure_unit, the levels of the disclosure unit, which are its members, also for a finding that lies on the rows of an assignment or of a pair (documents.md, Support). The filter applies these rules in the order of step 7, each with the detail 'secondary', which replaces the detail 'min_count' of a count below k apart from that of `support.contributors.distinct` (Steps, 3), rules 1, 2, 3 and 5 to every count that measures the quantity concerned, other than the counts of columns that measure the levels of the items of their terms with an offset, marked term_levels or table_term_levels (registry.md, Statistics and counts), to which rule 6 applies instead. They compare no count that measures no quantity with the suppressed quantities, whatever its value, since a suppression for an equal value would show that a suppressed quantity has that value, which written counts beside it could then single out: a median of 42 rows of 40 items, between a written lower quartile of 41 and upper quartile of 43, suppressed because 42 subjects have two rows or more would give back 3 subjects of one row beside 45 written subjects. A count that counts the same levels as a quantity only for some data, as varying_levels of structure.within_between counts the levels with at least two rows when the status is within, is a part of a count that measures that quantity, so part_of suppresses it. In the analysis sample, a count that names in part_of a person count that these rules suppress, directly or through another such count, is suppressed with it, since part_of writes a part of a person total only when the total exceeds it by k or more (Within a finding), so that the written part would bound the hidden quantity from below: all_zero_levels 40 of outcome.zero_shares beside its hidden levels would show 50 schools or more, beside df 49 the 50 schools of the design sample, so that none lacks an outcome.

In the analysis sample, a count that names in part_of a non-person count of levels that these rules suppress, directly or through another such count, is suppressed with the detail 'secondary' when it is not 0 and the levels of the item in the design sample exceed it by fewer than k, 0 included. ## Decisions

The finding status, the message identifier and the statistics of kind category and boolean are written as decided, because they state the finding itself and a report must state every finding (02-decisions.md, Q5). They can show that a suppressed count is 0 or lies in a range, such as at least 1 when keys.duplicates finds duplicates.

- For the T0 checks, which read no outcome, this is accepted. Their statuses and categories describe the structure of the design and the quality of the data, such as which grouping column is nested in which, whether a level is empty or whether a key repeats, and reports need them to interpret the model. ## Messages

A message in a shareable document is rendered from the written values (registry.md, Messages): the template when every statistic that it renders is written, and the fallback otherwise. A fallback renders no statistic that can be suppressed, so no message holds a suppressed value, '<10' never appears in a message and a message cannot disclose a value by contrast with another part of its text. Reports, which M4 builds, show a suppressed count as '<' followed by k when its detail is 'min_count', which marks only values below k, and as not shown otherwise. Labels in a message are rendered as their pseudonyms. Scope parts are names of columns, grouping factors, terms and units, which the filter keeps.

## Pseudonyms

A pseudonym replaces a label of a grouping factor or column in a shareable document (02-decisions.md, Q5), so that flagged clusters get labels that exist only in the report. Registry 0.1.0 has no statistic of kind labels, so its documents hold no pseudonyms, and these rules apply when an entry adds one.

- One pseudonym stands for one label of one grouping factor or column across the whole document, so that the same level gets the same pseudonym in every finding, and the same label of two factors gets two pseudonyms.
- The pseudonyms are '#1', '#2' and so on, numbered over the distinct pairs of a factor and a label that the document writes, in increasing order of the HMAC-SHA-256, under the commitment key of the run, of the canonical bytes of the array of the factor's name and the label (conventions.md, Keyed commitments), the digests compared as lower-case hexadecimal strings. The numbers therefore follow neither the order of the labels nor the order of the findings, and they are the same in both packages for the same key.
- The internal sidecar records each pseudonym with the factor, as the IR names it, and the label, as the data give it, in the order of the pseudonyms (documents.md, Internal sidecar).

## Guarantees

After the steps above, a shareable document meets the invariants of documents.md: step 3 gives E7020, step 2 E7021, steps 4 and 6 E7022, step 10 E7023 and step 5 E7024. Step 11 keeps the quantile of every statistic whose entry gives one, with the entry's p, and writes its n only beside its count, as E7041, E7045 and E7025 require of every document, so that the quantile rule that E7022 checks reaches every quantile. The document records the parameters of the registry, as E7046 requires, so that k in these invariants is the registry's.

The guarantees below read a document as its reader can: without its time of creation and the digests of its keyed commitments, and without the values that Decisions and this section accept to show: the statuses, messages, categories, booleans and names of the T0 checks, the scopes of the random terms that '||' makes of a frozen factor and, in a document that has such terms, `parameters` and `rank` of identifiability.gram_rank, which count their variances, the availability of a finding that is not available, the verdict of outcome.separation, with its status, message and both_classes, which the verdict and the counts that its finding writes give, and its suppression as a whole, the class of a by_unit count, the boolean dominated, and a quantile implied by a written count or shared by k or more of its values. A hidden value is a value that the document does not write and that no written count measures, and the values that the document leaves possible for it are those it takes in the tables, declarations and arguments whose documents read alike.

- Outcome-derived values. No hidden outcome-derived value has a single possible value, unless every table with the same predictors, keys, labels, grouping columns, assignments and declaration gives it one, as a difference of 0 does when the larger sample's quantity is 0, or gives it the value of a count that the document writes, as the levels of the analysis sample of a disclosure unit whose members each have one row take the value of its rows. The status and message of a finding of a T1 or T2 check suppressed as a whole are hidden values, the verdict of outcome.separation apart. A hidden outcome-derived count of persons, rows or levels whose detail is not 'min_count', 'min_count_rule' or 'dominance' has a possible value of k or more, unless the bounds that details state keep it below k, as they keep a part of a count shown below k, or rule 1 of Across findings hides it as a difference below k, which its firing displays as the detail 'min_count' would. The reader may use every value that the document writes, at either tier, every relation that the specifications fix between them, linear or not, the firing of every rule of this document, and the values that a predictor can take, though not which rows hold them. The reader also knows the declaration, whose hash the document writes unkeyed. The guarantee does not extend to a reader who knows from another source a design-structure value that the document hides: a reader who knows from a published protocol that each of 10 schools enrolled 10 pupils learns from 91 written analysed rows that 9 outcomes are missing, although rule 1 of Across findings hides the 100 rows of the design sample. Rule 1 would otherwise have to hide the analysed rows in almost every T1 or T2 document with few missing outcomes, and trial reports usually publish such counts of missing outcomes. Until the coordinator settles it (03-execution-plan.md, M2, Open items), the guarantee does not hold for one class of determinations, which the disclosure-attack tests list as a known channel: beside the firing of rule 1, which shows fewer than k rows without an outcome, a level known to hold k rows or more is shown not to have lost every outcome. It arises in ordinary crossed designs: with 20 subjects crossed with 12 items, one row in each combination and 3 outcomes missing, each subject holds 12 rows, so that none of the 20 analysed subjects that outcome.constant_clusters for 'item' writes as its contributors can have lost every outcome, and the subjects of the design sample, which rule 1 hides, are 20.
- Design-structure values. The values follow the rules of steps 2 to 5, and no hidden design-structure count has a single possible value under the linear relations of the document: the sums, differences and parts of totals that definitions state, the quantities that several counts measure, with the offsets by which the counts of columns measure the levels of the items of their terms where the formula fixes them, the same quantity in nested samples, the numerator and denominator of a ratio, the bounds that details state and the condition that every count is at least 0.

### Residual risk

The filter leaves these inferences about design-structure values open, as the output checking adopted on 8 November 2026 does, and the disclosure-attack tests report them without failing. Each applies only where rules 1 and 5 of Across findings do not hide the quantities concerned beside the analysis sample, where they would give back an outcome-derived difference.

- Products. With the relation crossed, `combinations` of structure.label_incidence is the product of `levels_first` and `levels_second`, so a suppressed count of the levels of one column can be given back from the combinations and the written levels of the other.
- Rows and levels. The rows of a sample hold one row of each level with one row and at least two of each other level, and at least each written quantile from its position on, so rows fewer than these bounds show that some level has one row, and so that the smallest size is 1: 21 rows of 12 levels hold at least 3 levels of one row. Likewise the members of a sample hold at least one member of each level of a factor constant within them, so that 3 subjects over the 3 levels of a factor between subjects hold one each.
- Quantiles pinned by rows and levels. The rows bound each quantile of the level sizes from above, and written singletons fewer than its position bound it from below by 2: 16 rows of 9 levels, 4 of them of one row, give a median of 2 rows at k = 4.
- Crossed designs. The rows of a level of one factor count the levels of another that it holds when no two rows share a level of both, so the quantiles of the level sizes of one factor bound the levels with one row of the other.
- Reals of counts. effective_size with the rows and the levels gives back the sum of the squared level sizes, which beside the quartiles gives back an extreme size of a factor whose sizes differ little, icc with within_share and the rows gives back the levels, and smallest_eigenvalue gives back the rows and the sizes of a factor with few levels.
- Reals over predictors. The standard deviations and condition number of design.scale_disparity, the GVIF of design.collinearity and within_share of structure.within_between give back, with the rows, how many rows hold each value of a predictor whose values a reader knows.
- Bounds that the firing of a rule implies. A count written in one sample shows that rule 1 did not fire against another, so that the other exceeds it by k or more.
- Non-person classes. Each level of an item whose counts resolve as non-person holds two members or more, which bounds the members and the rows of the sample from below.
- Covariance parameters. `parameters` of identifiability.gram_rank counts q(q + 1)/2 covariance parameters for a random term of q columns that estimates their correlations, so beside the other terms it gives back q, and with it the levels of a categorical variable of the term, through a relation that is not linear, and `rank` bounds it. The random terms that '||' makes of a frozen factor have a variance each, so `parameters` counts them linearly, as their scopes do (Decisions).
- Offsets that the data set. A count of columns measures the levels of the items of its terms with an offset that the formula fixes only in part, as the kept columns of earlier main effects that are constant within the levels of a factor lower df of design.collinearity, and the written counts can bound such an offset: budget of identifiability.df_budget lies between levels less kept of design.rank_deficiency and levels less 1, so that with 4 written levels, kept 2 and budget suppressed with the detail 'min_count' at k = 3, budget can only be 2, which shows that the covariate is constant within the levels.
- Thresholds of reals. The message of a finding compares a real with thresholds, so beside a written real from which a suppressed count follows it confines that count: gvif of design.collinearity with the message moderate, which shows that gvif_per_df, gvif to the power 1/df, reaches 5 but not 10, leaves df, which rule 6 suppresses, the single value 3 for a gvif of 133.25, and so gives back the 4 levels of a factor that step 3 suppresses.

The filter protects one document. Two shareable documents of the same data, such as a T0 document and a later T1 or T2 document, which the commitment to the analysis table lets a reader match (documents.md, Analysis table commitment), or two documents of tables that differ in a few rows, give back together the differences that rule 1 hides in each, such as the rows without an outcome as the rows of the T0 document minus those of the analysis sample of the T1 document. Only one of them should leave the analyst's machine, and the T0 findings of a T1 or T2 run replace the T0 document.

Two further channels are accepted within a document, besides those that Decisions accepts. The class person of a by_unit count shows that some level of its item holds at most one member, in the analysis sample too, since the document records the class that the filter applied (documents.md, Statistics). With the basis rows and the settings of registry 0.1.0, `dominated` true shows that a finding has at most two rows, whose count step 3 suppresses as below k. The class of a by_unit count of a finding of a T1 or T2 check is an outcome-derived value that the document writes, as documents.md requires. For the item of one of two crossed columns, whose every level holds every level of the other, that class would bound the rows that a level lost to missing outcomes from below, and Decisions suppresses the findings that would show it where a loss of fewer than k rows can leave a level with one member. The class non_person of a by_unit count, twice a written non-person count of levels whose item holds each member in one level, and `dominated` false bound the contributors of a finding below the minimum count from below, and Decisions suppresses such a finding where the bound would leave them one value.

A written count can still imply a suppressed quantile. When at least a quarter of the J levels of a factor have one row, `singletons` implies that `lower_quartile` of structure.cluster_sizes is 1, and `levels_without_minority` can imply that `median_minority` of outcome.minority_events is 0. The implied value is then shared by the levels that the written count counts, so it discloses nothing that the count does not, and the filter does not suppress the count. The written values can also give back a quantile that k or more of its n values share, such as the smallest size of a factor whose levels all hold the same number of rows. The value is then a property of k or more levels, as a written count of k or more is, and this is accepted. The quantile rule prevents a quantile from locating a value that fewer than k values lie on either side of.

The disclosure-attack tests of [sim/disclosure/](../../sim/disclosure/README.md) check these guarantees in CI: attacks (a) and (b) fail on an outcome-derived value with one possible value, on an outcome-derived count shown below k by the written values alone, and on a design-structure count that the linear relations alone determine, and report the other determinations of design-structure values as residual risk (03-execution-plan.md, M2).
