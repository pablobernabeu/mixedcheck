## Claims checked

1. 02-decisions.md, Q9: 'Failures and non-convergence count as outcomes and are never dropped (Pawel et al., 2026).'
   **Partly supported.** Section 6 asks researchers always to quantify, report and investigate missingness, to report how it was handled and why, and to pre-specify the handling approach for a more neutral evaluation. Section 4.1.1 treats quantifying and reporting missingness as a basic step, and Section 4.2.2 explains why method-wise deletion can make a method that fails on hard datasets look better than it is. The paper does not say that missing outputs must never be dropped. Method-wise and repetition-wise deletion are among the handling approaches it describes (4.2.2), with the advice to choose the approach to suit the study's aims and to run sensitivity analyses with alternative approaches (Section 6). 'Never dropped' is therefore this project's own, stricter rule and should not be attributed to the paper. The part that the paper supports is that failures and non-convergence are counted and reported as results and that the handling is fixed before the study runs.

2. open_items_local_session.csv: 'the protocol's missingness rules should follow the published wording'.
   **Noted for P1.** The published classification has three types: DGM missingness, method missingness and performance missingness (Section 3, Table 1). The handling approaches are, in the paper's order (4.2.1 to 4.2.9): non-analysis of conditions or methods with missingness, method-wise and repetition-wise deletion, additional repetitions, DGM adaptation, method adaptation, resource adaptation, method replacement, imputation and an alternative performance measure. The minimum the authors recommend is to quantify and report missingness and how it was handled, and to share code (Section 7).
