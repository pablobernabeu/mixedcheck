## Claims checked

1. open_items_local_session.csv: 'Detecting non-normal random effects; V2 module M02'. **Supported, with the paper's own recommendation.** The article shows that when the random effects follow a finite mixture of normal distributions, assuming normality can estimate them badly, and that the usual ways of inspecting the model's assumptions, such as plots of the empirical Bayes estimates, are not sound; it argues that the better way to detect mixture components is to build the mixture into the model (the heterogeneity model) and compare its fit with the Gaussian model (abstract, p. 217). For M02 this means that a check on the predicted random effects is weak evidence of non-normality, and that the paper's remedy is a model comparison.
2. review-catalogue.md (L5 entry on random-effect distributions) cites 'Verbeke & Lesaffre (1997)' for the consistency of ML estimates under non-normal random effects. **Not this paper.** That result is in a different article by the same authors, published in 1997; this 1996 article is about the heterogeneity model and the detection of mixture components. The two should not be conflated when the registry cites them.
