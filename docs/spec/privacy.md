# Privacy filter

The privacy filter turns the findings of a run into the findings of a shareable results document (02-decisions.md, Q5). Every file that leaves the package passes through it unless the caller asks for internal mode explicitly, and a shareable document meets the invariants of [documents.md](documents.md) (Shareable documents, E7020 to E7024). This document specifies its input, the order of its steps, how each rule of Q5 applies to a finding, how messages render, how labels become pseudonyms and which channels of disclosure by differencing it closes. It is normative together with documents.md and [registry.md](registry.md) (Statistics and counts, Messages), which also states how by_unit counts resolve. M2 applies it to the findings of the results document. M4 adds figures, their aggregated tables, rounding to `rounding_base` and the secondary suppression of tables, and extends this document.

## Parameters

The filter reads its parameters from the compiled registry (registry.md, The compiled registry) and the results document records them in `/privacy`. Below, k is `min_count`, the dominance rule has `dominance.contributors` contributors and the share m / 10^d written as `dominance.share`, and `zeros_disclosive` true means that a count of 0 is suppressed like any other count below k. The settings of registry 0.1.0 are k = 10, two contributors, the share 0.9 and zeros treated as disclosive, and every rule below that says '0 included' assumes zeros_disclosive true.

## Input

The filter receives the internal findings of the run in the order of documents.md (Findings), each with:

- the registry entry of its check
- its statistics with their internal values, every by_unit count already resolved to person or non_person (registry.md, Statistics and counts) and every quantile with its p and n
- its support, with the numbers behind the dominance rule: the rows of the sample and the summed rows c of the largest contributors
- the internal message, its identifier and the values of its arguments

and, for the document as a whole, the disclosure unit, the commitment key of the run and the counts of the samples that the rules across findings compare (Across findings). A finding that is not available passes through unchanged, also when the reason rests on a count that the rules across findings suppress, as Decisions accepts. The run resolves every by_unit count before the filter, in internal mode as well, so that every results document records the resolved class.

## Steps

The filter applies these steps in order. A value that a step suppresses becomes null with `availability` not_available, the reason privacy_suppressed and the detail given, and keeps its kind, count class and quantile. A value that an earlier step suppressed keeps its detail, apart from the detail 'min_count', which step 6 replaces where it would bound a suppressed value (Within a finding).

1. Internal statistics. Every statistic that its entry marks internal_only is suppressed, with the detail 'internal_only'.
2. Minimum count and dominance. A finding fails the minimum-count rule when its support has fewer than k distinct contributors, and the dominance rule when c × 10^d ≥ m × rows in integer arithmetic, with c the summed rows of its largest contributors (documents.md, Support). In a finding that fails either, every statistic of kind real, reals or labels and every count and ratio of class person is suppressed, with the detail 'min_count_rule' when the finding fails the minimum-count rule and 'dominance' otherwise (E7021). Counts and ratios of class non_person, booleans, categories and names stay. This step leaves the support to step 3.
3. Person counts below k. Every person count still written that is below k, 0 included, is suppressed with the detail 'min_count' (E7020): statistics of kind count with the class person, `support.rows` and `support.contributors.distinct`. A quantile that step 4 suppresses, every minimum and maximum among them, is left to that step, which suppresses it whatever its value, so that its detail does not show on which side of k it lies. A quartile of at most four values or a median of at most two is the minimum or the maximum, and its detail would otherwise show where that lies. So the detail 'min_count' marks only values below k, and every such value other than a quantile that the quantile rule suppresses and a value whose detail step 6 replaces.
4. Quantiles. A statistic with quantile p of n values, n being the internal value of the count that the entry names, is suppressed with the detail 'quantile' unless n × min(m, 10^d − m) ≥ k × 10^d in integer arithmetic, where m / 10^d is p (E7022). A minimum or a maximum, the quantile 0 or 1, is therefore never written.
5. Ratios. A ratio whose numerator or denominator is a person count that is suppressed or below k is suppressed with the detail 'ratio' (E7024).
6. Relations within a finding. The rules of Within a finding below are applied, and step 5 with them, until no value or detail changes.
7. Across findings. The rules of Across findings below are applied to the whole document, and step 6 is applied again to every finding that they change.
8. Absent statistics. A ratio that its definition leaves absent is written as suppressed, with the detail 'ratio', when its numerator or denominator is suppressed, and a quantile that its definition leaves absent is written as suppressed, with the detail 'quantile', when the count that gives its n is suppressed. A by_unit statistic written so takes the class to which that suppressed count resolves. Its absence would otherwise show that value, as the absence of constant_share of outcome.constant_clusters shows that multi_row_levels is 0. Entries define their other statistics so that their absence depends on no person count, as effective_size of structure.cluster_sizes, which is 'NaN' for a factor with one level.
9. Decisions. The rules of Decisions below may suppress a finding as a whole.
10. Pseudonyms. Every label still written is replaced by its pseudonym (Pseudonyms).
11. Writing. The quantile of a statistic keeps p and keeps n only when the count that gives n is written in the finding, so that n never shows a suppressed count. Every message is rendered again from the written values (Messages).

The filter never writes a value that the internal document does not hold, and it never changes a status other than availability.

## Within a finding

Three relations that an entry states let a written value give back a suppressed one within the finding (registry.md, Statistics and counts).

- suppress_with. A statistic whose suppress_with names a suppressed statistic is suppressed with the detail 'secondary'. It covers sums, differences and products, such as rows_kept and rows_dropped of sample.raw_vs_analysis, which add up to the written rows_raw. When a statistic that it names is suppressed with a detail other than 'min_count' and does not name it in its own suppress_with, the detail 'secondary' also replaces a detail 'min_count' that step 3 gave. A statistic so derived from another would otherwise show that it is below k while the detail of the other shows that step 3 found that one at least k: bound of identifiability.levels_vs_dimension, the levels minus 1, with the detail 'min_count' beside levels suppressed with 'secondary' would give the levels as 10. Two statistics that name each other, such as rows_kept and rows_dropped, are parts of a total, so that the detail 'min_count' of one bounds the other only to within k of the total, and they keep their details.
- part_of. A count whose part_of names a total counts a part of it, and the total minus its parts counts the rest, such as the counts other than 0 beside zeros of outcome.zero_shares. When the total is a person count, every count that names it in part_of is suppressed with the detail 'secondary' if the total minus one of them that steps 1 to 5 left written, or minus the sum of all of them that steps 1 to 5 left written, is below k, 0 included. The rule reads these internal values whether or not a later rule suppresses the total or a part, so that its result does not depend on the order of the rules, as for the nested totals multi_row_levels, varying_levels and informative_levels of structure.within_between. Such a remainder has the class of the total, so a non-person total is shown with its parts exactly.
- Quantiles. A statistic with quantile whose n is given by a suppressed count is suppressed with the detail 'quantile', since the quantile rule cannot then be checked from the document (E7022). The detail 'quantile' also replaces a detail 'min_count' that step 3 gave, since step 3 leaves to step 4 only the quantiles that the quantile rule suppresses, so that 'min_count' would show that the rule held and bound n: a median of structure.cluster_sizes with the detail 'min_count' would show that the factor has at least 20 levels.

## Across findings

A document can give back a suppressed count through values that different findings write, since several checks count the same rows, members or levels in different samples. These rules read the counts of the samples, not the values that other findings write, so that the shareable form of a finding depends only on its own internal values and on the samples.

The samples are the table, the design sample, the analysis sample when the run read the outcome, and the rows of each assignment (registry.md, Terms) and of each pair of columns that structure.label_incidence and structure.id_scope_evidence read, those of the design sample in which both columns are present, on which a check yields an available finding, since no finding writes a quantity of other rows. The rows of an assignment or of a pair lie in the design sample. They are the design sample when they hold the same rows as it, and otherwise the analysis sample when they hold the same rows as that, and the rows of assignments and pairs that hold the same rows as one another are one sample, since a sample with the same rows gives back nothing that it does not. So rule 1 compares two samples with the same rows only among the table, the design sample and the analysis sample. The quantities of a sample are its rows, its contributors as documents.md (Support) counts them and, for each item whose counts of levels resolve as person counts in the sample, the number of its levels, of its levels with at least two rows and of its levels with one row. The items are the grouping factors of the IR, the declared units that do not count as absent and the items by which the by_unit counts of the run's findings resolve (registry.md, Statistics and counts), so that other columns and combinations, such as the observation key and the outcome, are not items. Two items are one item when they divide the rows of the table into the same levels, as the factor school:class, the unit class declared within_parent in school and the pair of the grouping columns school and class do.

A count measures a quantity when its entry says so in measures (registry.md, Statistics and counts). `support.rows` measures the rows of the sample that the check's entry names, and `support.contributors.distinct` its contributors and, with the basis grouping_factors, the levels of every grouping factor whose number of levels in that sample equals it, also for a finding that lies on the rows of an assignment or of a pair (documents.md, Support). The filter applies these rules in order, each with the detail 'secondary', rules 1 and 2 to every written count that measures the quantity concerned.

1. Samples in turn. Every two samples of which one holds the other's rows are compared. When a quantity other than the levels with one row exceeds the same quantity of the smaller sample by fewer than k, 0 included, one of the two samples loses its value: the rows of an assignment or of a pair when the other is the table, the design sample or the analysis sample, and otherwise the larger sample, which for two of these three that hold the same rows is the one named first. A quantity of an item is compared when the item's counts of levels resolve as person counts in the larger sample, which holds the levels that the difference counts, whichever sample loses its value, and the levels of the item in the smaller sample are counted whatever their class there. When no level of the larger sample holds at most one member, every level that the difference counts holds two or more, so the difference is not a person count. The levels with one row are left out because a level with two rows in the larger sample can have one row in the smaller, so that the smaller sample can have more of them. The difference counts the rows, members or levels that a missing predictor, assigned column or outcome removes, which sample.raw_vs_analysis suppresses when it counts rows below k. Of the table, the design sample and the analysis sample, the larger loses its value because the smaller is the base of more statistics, such as the shares of the T1 checks, and the rows of an assignment or of a pair lose theirs because only the checks that read them count them. The table loses its values beside a design sample with the same rows, although the message complete of sample.raw_vs_analysis then shows that no row leaves the design sample, because that message is not written when a contrast's reference level that only the declared categories name has no row: the finding then has the message reference_emptied, whose fallback hides that rows_dropped is 0, and the rows of the table beside those of the design sample would show it. The levels of the combinations of two columns that structure.label_incidence finds crossed in a sample are the product of the levels of the two columns there, so when they lose their value in a sample in which the levels of both columns keep theirs and are not person counts below k, the levels of the combinations in the other sample of that comparison lose their value too.
2. Levels with one row. For each item and sample, the levels are the sum of the levels with at least two rows and the levels with one row, and each level with one row is one row, so that the levels with one row are a person count. When the levels with one row number fewer than k, 0 included, or when rule 1 suppresses the levels, every count of the levels with at least two rows in that sample is suppressed, and when rule 1 suppresses the levels with at least two rows, every count of the levels with one row is suppressed, so that no two of the three give back the third. The rule applies to items whose levels resolve as person counts only: every level of a non-person item holds at least two members and so at least two rows, as its class already shows.
3. Equal values. When rule 1 or 2 suppresses a quantity of a sample, every written count of a finding on that sample that has the same value and measures no quantity is suppressed too, unless its class, as resolved for the finding, is non_person, because it may count the same rows, members or levels. The findings of the assignment checks are on the rows of their assignment, and those of structure.label_incidence and structure.id_scope_evidence on the rows of their pair. For example, cells of identifiability.residual_confounding counts the levels of the factor of a term with an intercept alone and replicated_cells its levels with at least two rows. varying_levels of structure.within_between equals multi_row_levels when the status is within. A count whose quantities all stay written keeps its value, since it counts them whatever value another quantity shares with it. Counts of the levels or rows of another item are suppressed too, since an item can count the members of another: where subjects and items are crossed and the key has no duplicates, the rows of an item count its subjects, so a lower quartile of 60 rows for 40 items beside 60 subjects shows that at least 30 items hold every subject, so that no subject has one row, which the suppressed levels of subject with at least two rows hide.
4. Rows without a key value. missing_key_rows of keys.duplicates counts the rows of the table with a missing value in a column of the observation key. For each sample other than the table, when these rows and the rows of the table that the sample leaves out, one of which holds the other, differ by fewer than k rows, 0 included, missing_key_rows is suppressed, since beside the rows of the table and of the sample it would give back the rows by which they differ, such as the rows with a key value that a missing predictor removes from the design sample when a column of the key is a grouping column.
5. Reals over the design sample. When rule 1 suppresses the rows of the design sample, every statistic of kind real or reals of a finding on the design sample is suppressed, and when it suppresses the levels or the levels with at least two rows of an item in the design sample, so is every such statistic of a finding on the design sample whose scope names that item as its factor. The design sample loses a quantity under rule 1 only beside the analysis sample, so the rule applies only when the run read the outcome. There such a real, beside the counts of the analysis sample that the T1 findings write, can give back the rows or levels without an outcome that rule 1 hides: the standard deviation of a predictor with two values in design.scale_disparity takes one value for each number of rows that hold each value, which with the rows of the analysis sample can single out the rows of the design sample, and within_share of structure.within_between, with one row for each level and value of a predictor with two values, gives back the levels.

Rules 1, 2 and 4 compare quantities that the run computes from the data, whether or not a finding writes the smaller one.

## Decisions

The finding status, the message identifier and the statistics of kind category and boolean are written as decided, because they state the finding itself and a report must state every finding (02-decisions.md, Q5). They can show that a suppressed count is 0 or lies in a range, such as at least 1 when keys.duplicates finds duplicates.

- For the T0 checks, which read no outcome, this is accepted. Their statuses and categories describe the structure of the design and the quality of the data, such as which grouping column is nested in which, whether a level is empty or whether a key repeats, and reports need them to interpret the model. A status that shows a count to be 0 also gives back a value suppressed only because the difference was 0, such as identical_keys of keys.duplicates beside duplicated_keys when no key conflicts, rows_raw of sample.raw_vs_analysis with the message complete or varying_levels of structure.within_between with the status within, and this is accepted on the same ground. Their names, such as the factors that lose a level in sample.raw_vs_analysis and the columns that design.rank_deficiency drops, which can locate an empty cell of predictors, are accepted on the same ground, since vocabulary 0.1 cannot mark a predictor as a person attribute. So are their reals over predictors, such as the standard deviations and the condition number of design.scale_disparity, the GVIF of design.collinearity and within_share of structure.within_between, which with the rows can give back how many rows hold each value of a predictor with two values, also when the values are not 0 and 1. In a document whose run read the outcome, these reals could also give back the rows or levels without an outcome, so rule 5 of Across findings suppresses them wherever rule 1 hides those. The statuses, categories and availability of the T0 findings on the design sample stay written there, although beside the counts of the analysis sample they can show that the rows or levels without an outcome are 0 or lie in a range: the availability too_large of identifiability.gram_rank, for instance, shows whether the columns of Z, which a random intercept makes as many as the levels of its factor in the design sample, exceed max_z_columns when the levels of the analysis sample lie just below it. This is accepted, since such a value rests on a threshold and shows the difference only when a count of the analysis sample lies within k of one, and a report must state every finding.
- For the T1 and T2 checks, the counts are tabulations of the outcome, so a decision that rests on a suppressed person count is a cross-tabulation of a person attribute, which Q5 treats as disclosive even at 0. A finding of a check of tier T1 or T2 is suppressed as a whole when a statistic that a rule of its decision reads (registry.md, Decision), in the rule that held or in any rule before it, or in any rule when none held, is a count or a ratio of class person and is suppressed. Such a finding becomes not_available with the reason privacy_suppressed and the detail 'decision', keeps its scope and loses its message, statistics and support (documents.md, Findings).
- The verdict of outcome.separation states whether a linear combination of the fixed effects has events on one side only. Its involved columns, bounds and optimum are internal_only, so the shareable finding locates no cell, and so is its number of kept columns, which beside the kept columns of the design sample would show that the rows without an outcome emptied a cell of the predictors. Its first rule reads the numbers of events and non-events, so a finding with fewer than k of either, 0 included, is suppressed as a whole by the rule above, and its message one_class never shows that a class is empty. For the family class binomial, a row counts in events when it has an event and in non_events when it has a non-event, so that rows − events, rows − non_events and events + non_events − rows, with the rows of `support.rows`, count the rows without events, the rows without non-events and the rows with both. When one of these is below k, 0 included, the filter suppresses the finding as a whole with the detail 'decision'. It does so too, whatever the verdict, when the design sample or the analysis sample on which the check runs has at most two kept columns, as kept of design.rank_deficiency counts them in the design sample, or 0 without its finding, when its kept columns other than the intercept belong to fixed terms that read one variable alone, as the indicators of one factor do, or when two of its rows hold equal values in every kept column. A verdict of quasi or complete would then locate values or combinations of values of the predictors in which the outcome takes one value. With one variable, quasi with two kept columns names a cell, and complete shows that the events lie at one end of its values, or, for a factor, that each of its levels holds only events or only non-events, which the events and the level sizes can tell apart. When two rows share the values of the kept columns, complete shows likewise that each combination of values that occurs holds one class, and the events and the sizes of the combinations can show which, as for two factors of two levels whose main effects separate one combination from the other three, or a predictor of three values with its square, and quasi shows it for the combinations off its hyperplane. So the suppression shows nothing about the verdict. The rules read the analysis sample as well as the design sample, since a column that varies only in rows without an outcome leaves the analysis sample fewer kept columns. Such a suppression can then show that the rows without an outcome removed the columns of every variable but one, or made two rows share their values, and this is accepted: it shows where the outcome is missing, never an outcome value, and no rule that reads the design sample alone can tell which rows will lack an outcome. Otherwise, with kept columns from two or more variables whose values differ between every two rows, the verdict is written. The verdict complete then shows only that a hyperplane has every event on one side, and, as each combination of values is held by one row, it locates no combination that holds several rows of one class. Beside the events and the predictor values of every row, it can still narrow down the rows that hold events, most when the kept columns other than those of one variable vary in few rows, and this is accepted, since the verdict is the finding that a report must state.

## Messages

A message in a shareable document is rendered from the written values (registry.md, Messages): the template when every statistic that it renders is written, and the fallback otherwise. A fallback renders no statistic that can be suppressed, so no message holds a suppressed value, '<10' never appears in a message and a message cannot disclose a value by contrast with another part of its text. Reports, which M4 builds, show a suppressed count as '<' followed by k when its detail is 'min_count', which marks only values below k, and as not shown otherwise. Labels in a message are rendered as their pseudonyms. Scope parts are names of columns, grouping factors, terms and units, which the filter keeps.

## Pseudonyms

A pseudonym replaces a label of a grouping factor or column in a shareable document (02-decisions.md, Q5), so that flagged clusters get labels that exist only in the report. Registry 0.1.0 has no statistic of kind labels, so its documents hold no pseudonyms, and these rules apply when an entry adds one.

- One pseudonym stands for one label of one grouping factor or column across the whole document, so that the same level gets the same pseudonym in every finding, and the same label of two factors gets two pseudonyms.
- The pseudonyms are '#1', '#2' and so on, numbered over the distinct pairs of a factor and a label that the document writes, in increasing order of the HMAC-SHA-256, under the commitment key of the run, of the canonical bytes of the array of the factor's name and the label (conventions.md, Keyed commitments), the digests compared as lower-case hexadecimal strings. The numbers therefore follow neither the order of the labels nor the order of the findings, and they are the same in both packages for the same key.
- The internal sidecar records each pseudonym with the factor, as the IR names it, and the label, as the data give it, in the order of the pseudonyms (documents.md, Internal sidecar).

For example, with the key 00 01 02 … 1F, the labels 'A', 'B' and 'C' of the factor 'school' have the messages `["school","A"]`, `["school","B"]` and `["school","C"]` and the digests below, so that 'C' becomes '#1', 'A' '#2' and 'B' '#3'.

## Guarantees

After the steps above, a shareable document meets the invariants of documents.md: step 3 gives E7020, step 2 E7021, steps 4 and 6 E7022, step 10 E7023 and step 5 E7024. Step 11 keeps the quantile of every statistic whose entry gives one, with the entry's p, and writes its n only beside its count, as E7041, E7045 and E7025 require of every document, so that the quantile rule that E7022 checks reaches every quantile. The document records the parameters of the registry, as E7046 requires, so that k in these invariants is the registry's. The further suppressions of steps 1 and 6 to 9 and the IR hash of documents.md (inputs) close channels that the invariants alone leave open:

The filter protects one document. Two shareable documents of the same data, such as a T0 document and a later T1 or T2 document, which the commitment to the analysis table lets a reader match (documents.md, Analysis table commitment), or two documents of tables that differ in a few rows, give back together the differences that rule 1 hides in each, such as the rows without an outcome as the rows of the T0 document minus those of the analysis sample of the T1 document. Only one of them should leave the analyst's machine, and the T0 findings of a T1 or T2 run replace the T0 document.

Two further channels are accepted within a document, besides those that Decisions accepts. The class person of a by_unit count shows that some level of its item holds at most one member, in the analysis sample too, since the document records the class that the filter applied (documents.md, Statistics). With the basis rows and the settings of registry 0.1.0, `dominated` true shows that a finding has at most two rows, whose count step 3 suppresses as below k.

A written count can still imply a suppressed quantile. When at least a quarter of the J levels of a factor have one row, `singletons` implies that `lower_quartile` of structure.cluster_sizes is 1, and `levels_without_minority` can imply that `median_minority` of outcome.minority_events is 0. The implied value is then shared by the levels that the written count counts, so it discloses nothing that the count does not, and the filter does not suppress the count. The quantile rule prevents a quantile from locating a value that fewer than k values lie on either side of.

Disclosure-attack tests in CI, which M2c writes, check these channels on the pathology zoo (03-execution-plan.md, M2).
